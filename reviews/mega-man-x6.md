<h1>Mega Man X6</h1>

<p>
If X5 was fine, this is a clear step below it.
</p>

<p>
Let’s start with the positives, because there are some. The music is genuinely great, the core game feel is still solid, and the story is at least mildly interesting on paper. I’d even say about a third of the bosses are… fine. Not great, but serviceable.
</p>

<p>
Everything else though? Oh my god.
</p>

<p>
The level and boss design feel almost intentionally hostile. Visibility is constantly taken away, instant-death spikes are everywhere, and the game introduces rescuable NPCs that can permanently die due to awful placement. If one dies, your options are to restart the entire stage or give up entirely, because continuing means they’re gone forever. It’s frustrating in a way that feels unfair rather than challenging.
</p>

<p>
The story is another mixed bag. While the premise is somewhat interesting, it retcons a ton of established material, and the presentation is awful. There’s only Japanese voice acting, which is fine, but half the time the voice lines just cut out mid-sentence. You don’t even get to hear full lines, which completely kills any dramatic impact.
</p>

<p>
Boss design is one of the biggest offenders here. Several bosses are deeply unintuitive, and some can’t even be attacked normally. Instead, you’re forced into weird gimmicks just to interact with them, and it feels terrible. These fights aren’t clever or engaging, they’re just annoying and opaque.
</p>

<p>
The Sigma stages somehow manage to be even worse. Shadow gimmicks are overused and irritating, equipment requirements often make little sense, and a lot of mechanics feel half-baked or poorly explained. It’s honestly baffling that Capcom released the game in this state.
</p>

<p>
There is a weird “so bad it’s kind of funny” enjoyment if you push through it, but that novelty wears thin fast. It’s not enough to save the experience.
</p>

<p>
Zero also felt noticeably worse to play here. I never got his Black Armor, but even so, he never really caught up to X in terms of usability. Maybe there’s depth there that I didn’t fully engage with, but in practice, playing Zero felt like a constant struggle.
</p>

<p>
Overall, X6 feels messy, frustrating, and poorly thought out. Whatever good ideas it had are buried under punishing design choices and broken execution. Easily one of the weakest entries in the X series for me.
</p>
