<h1>Mega Man X5</h1>

![Mega Man X5 image](/assets/reviews/mega-man-x5/0f8b2463ff828decb42e7864468e5cb2.png)

Action Platformer · PS1

<p>
Mega Man X5 is… fine. It’s a slightly above-average game overall, but as a Mega Man X entry, it lands squarely in “average.”
</p>

<p>
The controls and general game feel are great. Both X and Zero handle well, movement feels responsive, and ducking in particular feels surprisingly good. From a pure mechanical standpoint, the game plays nicely.
</p>

<p>
Where it really stumbles is level design. Some stages, especially Dark Necrobat and the Sigma stages, are genuinely awful. They’re frustrating, poorly paced, and just not fun to play, which is especially disappointing this late into the X series.
</p>

<p>
Balancing is also strange. This might be the least punishing Mega Man X game overall. You technically have lives, but when you game over, you respawn at the same checkpoint anyway, which makes the entire system feel pointless. Most bosses are also extremely easy. Outside of one standout exception, I was able to face-tank damage and brute-force fights without much thought. I don’t mind an easier Mega Man game, but it’s still very noticeable.
</p>

<p>
The countdown system is another mixed element. Every mission ticking down a timer made me less comfortable experimenting with stage order and discouraged exploration. It didn’t end up mattering much mechanically, but psychologically it was a negative for me. Some players might like the added pressure, but it didn’t work in my favor.
</p>

<p>
One genuine improvement is the ability to choose between X and Zero before starting a mission. That’s a great change and something the series should’ve done sooner.
</p>

<p>
Overall, Mega Man X5 is a mediocre X game. It’s not bad, but it’s hard to recommend unless you’re specifically looking for more Mega Man X content.
</p>
